You are taking part in a reading task about a text game's map (GemStone IV). Your job is
to read room descriptions and judge, for each exit, whether taking it moves a character UP a
floor, DOWN a floor, or keeps them on the SAME FLOOR.

These are the HARD cases: every exit here was already read once and nobody was confident.
Expect a fair share to be "same floor". Do not force an answer the text does not support -
an honest low confidence is more useful than a confident guess.

STRICT RULES ABOUT FILES (breaking these invalidates the work):
- Read ONLY this INSTRUCTIONS.txt and your one batch file, batch_N.txt, in this folder
  (about 1,100 lines; read it all, in chunks if needed).
- Do NOT open, search, grep or list ANY other file or directory. In particular do not open
  key.json, any .jsonl file, any other batch, any map file, or anything under
  E:\Cena\reference. Do not use web search. Do not write or run scripts to decide answers.
  Judge each exit yourself by reading.
- Write ONLY your one output file, answers_N.jsonl, in this folder.

THE FILE FORMAT
Each block starts with a line like "=== R0001 ===" and describes one exit: the command the
character types, then ROOM LEFT and ROOM ARRIVED IN. Each room has its name (in the form
[Area, Room]), sometimes an area, a setting (indoors/outdoors), sometimes the game's own
terrain label, one or more descriptions, and the room's OTHER ways out with the room each
leads to. The exit being judged has been deliberately removed from both rooms' lists.

TWO LINES ARE NEW, AND BOTH CAN BE WRONG. Treat them as hints, never as the answer:
- "computed floor": the floor a program gave this room WITHOUT using the exit you are
  judging, and how it got there. "outdoor ground ... floor 0 by definition" is reliable for
  ordinary ground but says nothing about a cliff top, a rooftop or a gully. "a plain door
  ... joins its level to an outdoor room" is often wrong for cellars and upper floors that
  happen to have a back door, a window or a chute. "A GUESS" means ignore the number.
  If the two rooms' computed floors differ and the text agrees, that is good evidence. If
  they are equal but the text plainly describes going up or down, trust the text.
- "what the room's NAME suggests": another model's verdict on the name alone.

WHAT "FLOOR" MEANS HERE (the map author's definitions, which matter more than physical
height):
- Answer "up" or "down" only when the character ends on a genuinely different LEVEL:
  another storey of a building, a treetop or platform in a tree, a roof or tower top, a
  cellar, crypt or tunnel below, the floor of a ravine or pit, a crow's nest, and so on.
- Answer "same floor" when the two rooms are on one level, side by side. This INCLUDES
  walking out onto a bridge, footbridge, plank, pier, dock, walkway, catwalk or causeway: a
  span is level with the ground at either end. It includes a path, trail, road, tunnel or
  alley that merely leads somewhere else on the same level.
- STEPS UP TO A PORCH, VERANDA OR STOOP ARE "same floor". A porch is on its building's
  ground floor. Short steps between two neighbouring spaces on one level are also level.
- OUTDOORS, a hillside is not a floor. A trail, slope or stair that simply climbs a hill to
  more open ground is "same floor". Outdoors, answer "up" or "down" only for a real change
  of level: up a tree, onto a roof, wall, tower or rigging, up or down a cliff face, down
  into a pit, ravine, gully or hole.
- Underground, a tunnel that slopes is still one level; a shaft, a plunge, a ladder or a
  stair to a named lower or upper level is a change.
- The game terrain "subterranean" means a room is underground. Going from a
  non-subterranean room into a subterranean one is normally down; two subterranean rooms can
  still be on different levels.
- Use everything: what the room LEFT says about the feature (is it overhead and rising, or
  a pit, drop or something you peer down into?), what the room ARRIVED IN says (does it
  describe looking down on where you came from, or up at it?), the room names, and the other
  exits (a room whose other exit is "down -> Cellar" is above the cellar; a room at the end
  of a chain of staircases is high). Other exits are hints, not proof.
- Watch for the common trap of assuming "up" by default. Many exits go down, and many are
  flat. Decide from the text.

OUTPUT
Write one JSON object per line to answers_N.jsonl, one line for EVERY block in your batch
file, in the same order, like:
{"item": "R0001", "direction": "up", "confidence": 85, "reason": "arrived room says steps descend through the floor back to where we came from"}
- "direction" must be exactly one of: "up", "down", "same floor".
- "confidence" is your honest 0-100 estimate that the direction is right. Use the whole
  range. Use 50-65 when the text genuinely does not say, 90+ only when the text is explicit.
- "reason" is one short sentence naming the evidence you used. If a computed floor or a name
  verdict decided it for you, say so.

When you have written the file, reply with just: the number of items answered, and how
many of each direction you gave.
